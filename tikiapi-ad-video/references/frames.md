# Frames

One first frame per beat. The clip opens exactly on it, so everything the viewer needs to see must already be in it:
the people, the props, the set, the light and the look.

## Rules

- **9:16, 2K**, `nano-banana-pro`, at most 2 references (the character's face close-up plus one: the full body, a
  cast photo, a creature's front view, or a prop).
- **Framing first.** "Medium close-up from the chest up, her face large in frame", then the action, then the set in
  one clause. A long room description pulls the shot wide.
- **The look last**, the same words in every frame: "Look of a modern British crime comedy: low-key, two-thirds of the
  frame in deep shadow; rich black shadows, neutral, never green; mids warm cognac; highlights soft golden amber,
  never clipped; light from small warm tungsten lamps, hard and from the side; fine 35mm grain. No watermark, no logos."
- **No text in the picture**, unless it is printed on a costume and must stay readable. Captions, cards and stamps go
  on in the edit.
- **Open eyes where the edit freezes** (name cards usually sit on a freeze). A blink frozen on screen looks like a
  mistake.
- **Keep the stamp zones clear of faces:** the top fifth and the band above the bottom fifth will carry text.
- **Props are countable and tagged** when the line counts them: "exactly four small film reels in a row, each with a
  paper tag '30 s'".
- **Props have an owner:** "the tiki mug belongs only to the friend; it is the only drink in the shot".
- **Spell out colours that drift:** "bright neon lime-green fur like a highlighter, never dark green".

## A frame prompt

```text
IDENTITY She wears the outfit from image 2. Medium close-up from the chest up, her face large in frame: she sits on
the burgundy leather sofa and stares straight into the lens, completely deadpan, eyes open. Night, a flat with deep
emerald walls and brass lamps. LOOK
```

## A storyboard board (optional)

A 3×3 board of the whole ad on one image helps the person approve the story before the frames. Ask for "one 3x3
visual storyboard grid, read left to right and top to bottom, nine vertical 9:16 panels with thin dark dividers", then
one line per panel. Use it to approve the story; make the real frames one by one.

## Checking a frame

Against the character's face close-up: face, hair, build. Against the beat: the action is readable in a still; the
props are all there and countable; the eyes are open if it freezes; no face in a stamp zone; no stray text.
