# The beat table: rules and a worked example

## Why each rule exists

These come from a 36-second ad that went through nine script versions and several cuts before every test viewer got it.

| Rule | What went wrong without it |
|---|---|
| One idea per ad | Early drafts mixed "cheaper", "no subscription", "what the model can do" and a heist. Nobody could say what the ad was about |
| 2.5–3.5 s per beat | With beats under 2 s the first viewer said "everything happens too fast, nothing is clear". At about 3 s every beat landed |
| Never speed up the voice | A 1.25× read fit the cut but was too fast to follow. A natural-pace read with fewer words worked |
| Roles, not names | "Kate", "Raven": viewers spent the time learning names. "This creator", "her friend" needed nothing |
| Lines that work by ear | "Bye-bye, monster" and "two-fifty" meant nothing to the audio-only listener. "Bye-bye, subscription" and "two dollars fifty" did |
| Show the maths | "$12 a video" alone left viewers asking where it came from. "$49 ÷ 4 videos =" then the stamp answered it |
| Props physically logical | The kid asked whether the four videos were 30 s too. Tagging each reel "30 s" and counting them 1-2-3-4 on screen fixed it |
| Action in the first second | The muted viewer swiped at 5 s on two freeze frames. Opening on the monster gulping cash kept them |
| One text layer at a time | Subtitles, a name card and a stamp at once: too much to read in 3 s, and the subtitle covered the card |

## Example: "The Monthly Monster" (36 s, 9:16)

One idea: a subscription costs about $12 a video; per video it's $2.50, with no subscription. One video track serves
any language: no words inside the generated video, all text in the edit. ($2.50 was our launch price in November 2026: a
new script takes our price from `estimate_cost` on the day, and the rival's from its own page, dated.)

| # | Time | Picture | Narrator line | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3.8 | The creator on a sofa beside a furry lime monster; freeze on her open eyes | "This creator makes AI videos." | Name card: THE CREATOR · Makes AI videos. |
| 2 | 3.8–6 | Freeze on the monster | "This is her subscription." | Name card: THE SUBSCRIPTION · Eats $49. Every month. |
| 3 | 6–9 | She feeds it a wad of cash; it gulps | "She feeds it forty-nine dollars a month!" | Stamp: $49 / MONTH |
| 4 | 9–12.8 | It spits film reels onto the table; four reels, each tagged "30 s", counted 1-2-3-4 | "It gives her four thirty-second videos." | 1 2 3 4, then "4 videos · 30 s each" |
| 5 | 12.8–15.8 | Her deadpan stare into the lens | "That's twelve dollars a video!" | "$49 ÷ 4 videos =" then stamp "$12 A VIDEO" |
| 6 | 15.8–20.8 | Her friend sips from a tiki mug and holds up a phone with an epic dragon video | "Her friend pays per video." | Name card: THE FRIEND · Pays per video. |
| 7 | 20.8–24.5 | One coin spins and lands on the table | "The same thirty-second video on Seedance AI: two dollars fifty." | Stamp: $2.50 · ONE 30-SECOND VIDEO |
| 8 | 24.5–28.3 | The monster's tear; it packs a tiny suitcase and leaves; she waves | "No monthly fee. Bye-bye, subscription!" | Stamp: CANCELLED · No monthly fee. |
| 9 | 28.3–36 | End card: the logo, "$12 A VIDEO" struck through, "$2.50 A VIDEO", the address as a button | "Seedance AI video for two-fifty. Tiki A-P-I dot com." | Launch price note in readable text; the plan maths in a small line |

The rival's plan is recognisable from checkable facts (a $49 plan, 1,000 credits, 210 credits a 30-s video) without
its name, logo or colours.
